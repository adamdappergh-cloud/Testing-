# Props

The library, and the test for inventing one.

## The test

A prop earns its place when **its own conventions already perform the withholding**.

Run the withheld items against the candidate world and ask what that world calls each one. A classified file calls a secret venue `LOCATION: REDACTED` — the black bar is native, so it needs no explaining and invites tapping. A restaurant check has no native way to hide anything, so the same secret has to be bolted on and explained.

Three checks, in order:

1. **Does the form hide things already?** Redactions, sealed envelopes, scratch panels, a departures row still reading `———`.
2. **Does it have a native blank the recipient fills?** An order line, an agenda, a guest count, a form field. This is where their agency goes.
3. **Does it have a moment of finality?** A stamp, a gate closing, a signature, a receipt printing. The payoff needs somewhere to land.

A world scoring on all three needs almost no invention. One scoring on none will fight you the whole way.

Then: **does it fit the two of them?** A world that lands on a private joke, a shared profession or a running argument beats a better-scoring one that doesn't.

## The library

**Classified dossier** — `OPERATION [NAME]`, agent and handler, clearance, eyes only. Redaction bars are native, so secrets need no scaffolding; the recipient taps them and is refused. Self-destructing briefings make a countdown mean something. Strongest all-round scorer, and it recasts a couple slipping out for lunch as an operation with a window.

**Guest check / order docket** — table, covers, ruled lines, a total. Natively about food and choosing; the recipient's picks are literally an order. Weak at hiding — nothing on a check wants to be secret — so reach for it when the blank matters more than the secret.

**Departures board** — flight number, gate, `DEPARTS 12:30`, `DESTINATION ———`. The blank destination is the cleanest home there is for a withheld venue, and split-flap letters that never resolve are a set piece. Boarding passes issue at the end.

**Delivery tracking** — a progress bar, stops remaining, a driver who is the sender. Instantly legible on a phone and funny in two seconds, which no papery prop manages. Thin on romance, strong on recognition.

**Telegram** — `LUNCH CONFIRMED STOP 1230 HOURS STOP LOCATION WITHHELD STOP`. Reads in ten seconds. Almost no interaction surface: use it when brevity is the joke.

**Heist briefing** — the target, the window, the complications, a floor plan with a route. Scores like the dossier and lands harder on anyone whose free time is genuinely stolen from other obligations.

## Inventing one

Start from the recipient rather than the list. What form does **this person** handle often enough to recognise instantly — a prescription, a setlist, a match programme, a court listing, a lab result, a shipping manifest, a school report? Recognition in the first second is worth more than any amount of craft afterwards.

Then run the three checks. Where the world is missing one, borrow that single element from another and leave the rest alone — a stamp is portable; a whole second visual identity is not.

Avoid a world whose real-life instances carry bad news.
