Reference · Residential Space Planning

Glossary

The shared vocabulary used across the lessons. When a lesson uses one of these terms, it means exactly what's defined here.

Adjacency matrix
A grid listing every room on both axes; each cell records how close two rooms should be: ● adjacent, ○ nearby, · neutral, ✕ keep apart. Turns vague layout preferences into a spec. (Lesson 2½)
Bubble diagram
A freehand sketch of a plan as labelled circles, sized by importance and joined by lines for the adjacencies that matter — drawn before any rooms, to test a layout. Workflow: program list → sizes → adjacency matrix → bubbles → floor plan. (Lesson 2½)
Buffer
A space — hall, stair, wardrobe, or service room — placed between a private room and a public one so noise and traffic don't spill across. A single rung on the privacy gradient. (Lessons 2, 2½)
Circulation
The paths people take to move through a home: halls, stairs, and routes across rooms. Good circulation is short and clear, and never forces you through one room to reach another. (Lessons 1–2; expanded in Lesson 3)
Door swing (arc)
The quarter-circle on a plan showing the path a door sweeps as it opens. The floor inside the arc must stay clear of furniture and people. (Lesson 1)
Floor plan
A horizontal "slice" through a building at about chest height, seen from straight above and drawn to scale. (Lesson 1)
North arrow
The symbol fixing the plan's orientation. Find it first: from it you work out where the sun falls and which rooms get daylight. (Lesson 1)
Privacy gradient (a.k.a. intimacy gradient)
Christopher Alexander's Pattern 127: arrange spaces in a sequence so privacy rises steadily from the entrance inward, letting each visitor stop at the depth that fits them. The principle beneath "buffering." (Lesson 2½)
Scale
The fixed ratio between a drawing and reality — e.g. 1:50 means 1 cm on paper equals 50 cm in the building. Always confirm it before reading dimensions. (Lesson 1)
Zones — public / private / service
The three functional groups of rooms. Public: gather & receive (living, dining, kitchen, guest WC). Private: rest & retreat (bedrooms, en-suites, study). Service: the working spaces (laundry, utility, pantry, garage, storage). (Lesson 2)
