Form-Making  ·  Lesson 5

From Forces to a Parti

Fusing climate, roof, massing and edge into one whole-form idea

You now hold four artefacts: a force-map, a roof, a massing and a layered edge. This lesson fuses them. A parti (from the French parti pris) is the single organizing idea of a building — the diagram you can draw in a few strokes that captures the whole concept. Without one, a design is a pile of good moves; with one, the building reads as a single thought. By the end you'll have made your fifth artefact: one or two named parti concepts for the Pokuase site, each tested against the forces.

Tier 1 · Core One idea that holds it all A parti bundles every move under a single, drawable concept.

A parti is not more detail — it's less. It's the essential move that organizes everything else. You build one in five steps:

StepWhat you do
1 · Name the tensionRestate the site's core conflict in one line — usually "open to the SW breeze, defend the hot west sun, shed the rain, vent the heat."
2 · Choose the dominant movePick the one move that resolves most of it — often the roof or the courtyard. That becomes the spine of the idea.
3 · Bundle the rest under itHang the massing and the layered edge off that move so they all serve one concept.
4 · Name it & draw itGive it a short name and a five-stroke diagram — the parti. If you can't draw it small and simple, it isn't a parti yet.
5 · Test itTrace the sun and breeze over the diagram. Where does it win? Where does it strain? Refine, or try a second parti.

For the same Pokuase site, different dominant moves give different partis — and exploring one or two is exactly the point:

Two partis for one site (north up, so west is on the left), each a whole-form idea you can say in a sentence and draw in a few strokes. A: one great parasol roof floats over thin pavilions and vents at the top. B: rooms wrap a shaded, breathing courtyard. Both open to the SW breeze and screen the hot west.

Tier 2 · Deeper What does the building want to be? Kahn's search for the essential order, and the discipline of the parti.

The parti is old discipline and deep thinking at once.

Kahn — the essential idea

Louis Kahn famously asked of every project, "what does the building want to be?" — searching past fashion and convenience for the building's essential order, the one idea everything else should serve. His buildings are organized by a clear concept (served spaces and the "servant" spaces that support them). A parti is exactly that: the order found first, then honoured throughout.

The parti pris — decide the scheme first

The word comes from the Beaux-Arts tradition of the parti pris — "the stand taken." Before any detail, you commit to one organizing scheme in a quick diagram. The discipline is the point: a building with a clear parti makes every later decision easier, because they all answer to one idea.

One or two, then test

Good designers rarely marry their first idea. They sketch a couple of partis, each led by a different dominant move, and test each against the real forces — here, the sun path and the SW breeze. You keep the one that resolves the most tension with the least strain. That comparison is the design thinking.

Architecture is the thoughtful making of spaces.
— Louis Kahn

Tier 3 · Apply Draw your parti — one or two Your fifth artefact — the whole form in a sentence and a sketch.

Hands-on. Lay out your four artefacts (force-map, roof, massing, edge) and fuse them:

  1. Write the site's core tension in one line.
  2. Choose the dominant move that resolves most of it — roof, court or bar.
  3. Bundle roof + massing + edge under it as one idea.
  4. Name it (e.g. The Parasol, The Breathing Court, The Verandah Bar) and draw it in five strokes — your parti diagram.
  5. Do a second parti with a different dominant move.
  6. Test both: trace the sun (is the west defended?), the SW breeze (does it pass through?), the rain (does the roof shed it?), the heat (where does it vent?). Mark each parti's wins and strains.

Pick your lead parti — and keep the runner-up. The capstone shows both.
