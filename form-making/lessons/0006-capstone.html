Form-Making  ·  Capstone

A Portfolio of Climate-Driven Forms

Your five artefacts, gathered into three concept directions for the Pokuase site

This is the capstone. It gathers everything the series built — the force-map, the roof, the massing, the layered edge, the parti — into a small portfolio of climate-driven form concepts for the Pokuase site. These are concept directions to take to your architect: schematic, not to scale, and deliberately free of the existing concept plan. All three start from the same forces and answer the same tension — open to the SW breeze, defend the hot west sun, shed the rain, vent the heat — and differ only in the one dominant move that leads.

Every concept below is built from these five. Here they are, developed one more step.

A · The Parasol

One great roof over everything.

Concept A — section. One big roof, deep eaves and a vented ridge floats over thin volumes on a plinth. Sun caught by the eaves, rain thrown clear, hot air vented at the ridge, the SW breeze crossing underneath and through the gap. Leads with the roof.
Why it works
  • Sun: deep eaves and the high parasol shade the walls and openings all day.
  • Breeze: thin volumes lifted on a plinth let the SW monsoon cross underneath and between them.
  • Rain: the big pitched roof throws heavy downpours well clear.
  • Heat: the vented ridge and the open gap under the floating roof let hot air escape — stack ventilation.
Watch
  • A large roof is usually the costliest element — it has to earn its keep.
  • The open under-roof gap needs careful detailing against driven rain and insects.
  • It wants a generous plot to spread out and float.

B · The Breathing Court

Rooms wrap a shaded, breathing void.

Concept B — plan (north up, so west is on the left). Rooms wrap a shaded central court inside a veranda ring; the hot west gets a deep screened veranda; the SW breeze is drawn into the court, which vents hot air up. Leads with the massing — a modern Akan compound.
Why it works
  • Breeze: drawn in and channelled through the central court to every surrounding room.
  • Sun: the rooms shade the court and themselves; the hot west wears a deep, screened veranda.
  • Rain: roofs pitch to shed inward and out; the court is drained and planted.
  • Heat: the court behaves like a chimney, drawing hot air up and out.
  • Rooted: it is the local Akan / Asante courtyard compound, brought up to date.
Watch
  • More enclosure means more wall and roof per usable m².
  • The court must be sized to stay shaded yet bright — too small reads as a light-well, too big bakes.
  • Court drainage matters in the heavy season.

C · The Verandah Bar

A thin bar, deep verandas on both long faces.

Concept C — section. A single thin bar, one room deep, lifted on a plinth, with deep verandas on both long faces under a broad venting roof. The breeze crosses straight through and the eaves shade the verandas. Leads with the edge — verandas everywhere.
Why it works
  • Breeze: one room deep, so the SW monsoon cross-ventilates every room.
  • Sun: short faces to east/west; broad eaves and deep verandas shade the long faces; the west end is screened.
  • Rain: a single broad roof sheds simply and cheaply.
  • Heat: tall rooms vent at the ridge; the plinth lifts the floor off the damp.
Watch
  • A long thin bar eats site length and can feel corridor-like if it runs on too far.
  • It only works if the long axis really runs E–W — check it against your plot.
  • The west end still needs real screening, not just an eave.
