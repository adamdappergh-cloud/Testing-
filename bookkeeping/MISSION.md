# Mission: Church Bookkeeping

## Why
As a Deacon of a small reformed Baptist church in Accra, Ghana, you hold an ongoing fiduciary
responsibility for the congregation's money. Members entrust their tithes and offerings to the
church's care; accurate, transparent records are how you honour that trust, protect the church
from financial error or accusation, and give the elders the information they need to lead well.

## Success looks like
- Count and record every Sunday offering correctly, with two people present, within 24 hours
- Enter all income and expenses into a ledger weekly without error
- Reconcile the church bank account against the ledger every month
- Produce a monthly income-and-expenditure report for the elders
- Keep restricted funds (building, missions, benevolence) separate from general operating funds
- Present a clear annual financial statement to the congregation at the AGM
- Spot an irregularity when it appears and know how to raise it

## Context
- **Country:** Ghana — GHS (cedi) currency; GRA charitable-organisation framework applies
- **Church type:** Small local reformed Baptist congregation, Accra
- **Role:** Deacon with bookkeeping responsibility (volunteer, limited hours per week)
- **Starting point:** Zero prior bookkeeping or accounting knowledge

## Constraints
- Volunteer role — lessons must be short and immediately applicable
- Limited budget for software — start with paper ledger or Google Sheets; software optional later
- No paid staff assumed (payroll deferred until relevant)

## Out of scope
- Tax preparation (that is the accountant's job)
- Payroll processing (deferred — no staff currently)
- Advanced accounting software (Wave/Aplos introduced conceptually; deep training deferred)
- Commercial or business accounting
